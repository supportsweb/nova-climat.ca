Highly energy-efficient heat pumps

Cooling a home or commercial space is remarkably economical with a ductless or central heat pump. These systems provide excellent performance throughout both summer and winter.

Their secret lies in the way they operate: they can deliver up to four times more energy than the electricity they consume.

During cooling mode, heat pumps do not create cold air. Instead, they remove heat and humidity from every room and transfer it outdoors. Many models also help improve indoor air quality through advanced filtration.

Here's how the cooling process works.

1

The indoor unit contains refrigerant circulating through a coil. The fan draws warm indoor air across the cold coil, allowing the refrigerant to absorb the heat. Cooler air is then circulated back into your home.

2

As the refrigerant absorbs heat, it changes from a liquid into a gas and travels through copper lines to the outdoor unit.

3

In the outdoor unit, the compressor compresses the refrigerant gas, causing it to become extremely hot. The outdoor fan then releases this heat into the outside air.

4

The refrigerant cools, returns to its liquid state and flows back to the indoor unit, where the cycle repeats until your thermostat reaches the desired temperature.

Inverter technology for even greater energy savings

Older heat pumps maintained indoor temperature by constantly turning on and off, which consumed considerably more electricity.

Modern heat pumps feature Inverter technology, allowing the compressor to continuously adjust its operating speed based on the heating or cooling demand.

Instead of starting at full power every time, the compressor gradually increases its speed, reducing energy consumption during startup.

Once the desired indoor temperature has been reached, the compressor slows down to approximately 20% to 30% of its capacity to maintain a consistent temperature instead of shutting off completely.

Thanks to this variable-speed operation, heat pumps equipped with Inverter technology can reduce cooling and heating electricity costs by up to 50% compared to older systems, which typically provided energy savings of up to 40%.

It is a smart investment that continues to pay for itself over the long term.
